### FAQ list (10–15 Q&As) — drafted answers (careful legal language)

> **Legal tone for all answers:** Informational only; not legal advice; rules vary by jurisdiction and change over time; confirm with the local building department and your HOA/governing documents. FenceLine Florida is a fence installer, not a law firm or government agency.

1. **Do I need a fence permit in Florida?**  
   **A:** Often you might, but Florida does not use one single statewide rule for every fence. Whether a building permit is required depends on your city or county, the fence height and construction, lot conditions (such as corner lots or easements), commercial vs residential use, pool barriers, and other project details. HOA approval may still be required even when a city permit is not. Confirm with your local building department before installation.

2. **Is HOA approval the same as a building permit?**  
   **A:** No. A building permit is issued by a government building department. HOA or architectural review is a private approval under your community’s covenants. Many projects need one, both, or neither—depending on location and community rules. Completing only one does not automatically satisfy the other.

3. **Do I need a fence permit in Orlando?**  
   **A:** The City of Orlando publishes specific conditions when a fence permit is required (for example commercial property or residential with three or more units, height over six feet, corner lots, alleys, easements, pool barriers, certain materials, powered or vehicular gates, flood areas, and other listed situations). Even when a permit is not required, fences must still follow City Code, and HOA approval may still apply. Review the official City of Orlando fence permit page for the current list and applications.

4. **Do I need a fence permit in Jacksonville?**  
   **A:** Jacksonville and Duval County requirements can depend on property location and project scope. This page does not replace official city or county guidance. Contact the appropriate Jacksonville / Duval building services office for your address and confirm permit needs before you build. FenceLine Florida can help coordinate documentation for vinyl and chain-link projects we install in the Jacksonville market.

5. **Do I need a fence permit in Kissimmee?**  
   **A:** Kissimmee and Osceola County rules depend on whether the property is inside city limits or unincorporated areas and on the details of the fence. Confirm with the City of Kissimmee or Osceola County building department for your specific property. Many nearby communities also have HOA design rules that apply separately.

6. **Can I replace an existing fence without a permit?**  
   **A:** Not always. Some jurisdictions treat replacement similarly to new construction, especially if height, location, materials, or structural elements change, or if the original fence was noncompliant. Always verify with the local building department and your HOA before replacing a fence.

7. **What fence heights are allowed in Florida?**  
   **A:** Maximum heights and front-yard vs rear-yard rules are set locally by zoning and building codes—and often tightened further by HOAs. There is no single height that is always legal statewide. Check your zoning rules and HOA guidelines for your lot.

8. **Do HOAs allow vinyl fences in Florida?**  
   **A:** Many Florida HOAs allow or prefer vinyl privacy fences because they are low-maintenance and uniform in appearance—but each association sets its own standards for style, color, height, and placement. Approval is not guaranteed. Submit the materials your ARC requires and wait for written approval when your community requires review.

9. **Do HOAs allow chain-link fences?**  
   **A:** Some HOAs restrict or prohibit chain link, especially in front yards, while others allow it in rear or side yards or with black vinyl-coated mesh. Commercial properties may follow different standards. Review your HOA documents rather than assuming chain link is permitted.

10. **What if my property is in a historic district?**  
    **A:** Historic districts often require extra design review. For example, City of Orlando guidance indicates that plastic or vinyl fences are not permitted in historic districts and that a Certificate of Appropriateness may be required before installing or replacing a fence. Confirm historic status and approved materials with the local preservation/building process before ordering materials.

11. **Who is responsible for getting the permit—homeowner or contractor?**  
    **A:** Responsibility can be defined by local rules and your contract. Many homeowners hire a contractor that assists with applications and documentation. The property owner remains responsible for compliance with codes and HOA covenants. FenceLine Florida manages permitting and HOA documentation as part of our process for most residential and commercial jobs we install—without guaranteeing government or HOA outcomes.

12. **How long does fence permit or HOA approval take?**  
    **A:** Timelines vary widely. Some city processes advertise short processing windows for simple applications; HOA architectural committees may meet monthly. Build schedule buffers for revisions, neighbor notice periods, and inspections. Ask your city portal and HOA manager for current expectations.

13. **What happens if I install a fence without a required permit or HOA approval?**  
    **A:** Possible outcomes include stop-work notices, fines, required removal or modification, failed inspections, insurance complications, and HOA enforcement or resale issues. The safest approach is to confirm requirements first. This is general information, not legal advice.

14. **Does FenceLine Florida handle permits and HOA paperwork?**  
    **A:** Yes. FenceLine Florida manages permitting and HOA documentation as part of our process for most residential and commercial vinyl and chain-link installations in Jacksonville, Orlando, and Kissimmee. We do not provide legal advice and cannot guarantee that a city or HOA will approve any specific design.

15. **What materials does FenceLine Florida install?**  
    **A:** We specialize in vinyl (PVC) and chain-link fencing only—for residential and commercial properties in Jacksonville, Orlando, and Kissimmee. That focus supports clearer quotes, Florida-ready installs, and documentation packages that match the systems we actually build.
